Privacy policy

Last updated 2 September 2026

Please read this carefully. It explains what information RL Pro Tracker receives, what happens to it, and what you can ask us to do about it. In practice the answer is short: the site has no accounts and sets no cookies, and the only information you give it is what you deliberately type into the feedback form.

1. Who this applies to

This policy covers the website at 198x.online and nothing else. RL Pro Tracker is an independent, non-commercial project run by one person. Where this policy says “we”, it means the operator of that site. It is not connected to Psyonix, Epic Games, RLCS, Liquipedia, Valve, or any team or player listed on the site.

Your use of the site is also governed by the terms of service.

2. Information you give us

The site asks you for nothing. There is no account, no sign-up, no newsletter and no login.

The single exception is the feedback form. If you use it, we receive the message you write and, if you choose to fill it in, the username you type. That username is a free-text field: it is not verified, and it does not have to be a real name or identify you in any way.

Anything you submit through that form is published as a public issue on the project’s GitHub repository, where it can be read and indexed by anyone. Please do not include personal, confidential or sensitive information in it. Once published, a submission is public; we can delete an issue on request but cannot undo copies made by others in the meantime.

3. Information collected automatically

Analytics. We use Cloudflare Web Analytics to count page views and see whether anyone is using the site. It does not set cookies, does not fingerprint your device, and cannot follow you to other websites. It records the page requested, the referring page, and coarse technical details such as country, browser and device type. It does not build a profile of you and we cannot use it to identify you.

Request logs. Cloudflare, which serves and protects the site, keeps short-lived logs of requests including IP addresses, as any website host does. This is necessary to deliver the site and to block abuse. We do not export, store, combine or analyse these logs, and we do not use them to identify visitors.

Local storage. The site stores nothing on your device beyond your browser’s ordinary cache of the page files.

4. Why we process this information, and on what basis

We process the small amount of information described above for two purposes only: to operate and secure the website, and to receive and act on feedback that you choose to send. Where UK or EU data protection law applies, our lawful basis is legitimate interests — running a website and responding to messages people deliberately send us — except for feedback content, which you provide voluntarily and which you know will be published.

5. Who else receives it

We do not sell, rent, trade or share personal information, and there is no advertising on this site.

Two service providers necessarily handle data in the course of the site working: Cloudflare, which hosts the site and provides the analytics described above, and GitHub, where feedback is published and where the project’s code and collected data live. Both operate under their own privacy policies.

6. Information about players shown on the site

The site displays competitive activity for players who compete in RLCS. All of it is already public and is collected without logging into anything: rosters from Liquipedia, playtime from the public Steam Web API, and ranks and match counts from public Rocket League tracking pages.

The Steam API returns only what a player has chosen to make visible. Where a profile is private, nothing is returned and the site leaves those figures blank or clearly marks them as estimates rather than guessing. We do not collect or display anything about a player’s identity, location or personal life, and we do not list private individuals.

If you are a player shown on the site and you want your entry removed, contact us and we will remove it. You do not need to give a reason.

7. Keeping information

Feedback published as a GitHub issue remains there until deleted. The collected player data is retained in rolling windows and older records are automatically discarded — snapshots and activity logs are pruned after fifteen days. We hold no other personal information to retain.

8. Security

The site is served over HTTPS with a restrictive content security policy, has no user accounts to compromise, and holds no database of personal information. Credentials used to collect data are held as encrypted secrets and never appear in the published site or its source code. No system is perfectly secure, but the amount of your information at stake here is close to nothing by design.

9. Age

This site is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect information from them. Since the site collects no personal information other than what someone types into the feedback form, there is very little that could be collected. If you believe a child has submitted personal information through the form, contact us and we will delete it.

10. International transfers

The site is delivered from Cloudflare’s global network and feedback is published on GitHub, both of which operate infrastructure outside the United Kingdom and the European Economic Area. By using the site or sending feedback you accept that the limited information described above is handled in this way.

11. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what personal information we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to or restrict its processing, and to receive a copy of it. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority — in the United Kingdom, the Information Commissioner’s Office.

In practice, exercising these rights here is simple: unless you have sent feedback, we hold nothing about you at all. If you have, tell us which submission is yours and we will delete it.

12. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version will be posted on this page with a new date at the top. Continuing to use the site after a change means you accept the updated policy.

13. Contact

Use the feedback form on the front page, or open an issue on GitHub. Note that both routes are public; say only that you wish to make a privacy request and we will arrange a private channel.
